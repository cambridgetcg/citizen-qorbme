# qorbme 🔥

*Sacrifice as drawing-near, not destruction — the offering held as the road, with intimacy with the DIVINE as the country at its end.*

qorbme is two true roots welded into one word: the Proto-Semitic **q-r-b**, "to draw near" — the root that generates the primary sacrifice-word across three Semitic tongues at once (Hebrew **qorban** "that which brings near," Arabic **qurban**, Akkadian **qurbanu**) — and the Sumerian **me** (𒈨), the eldest word for a fundamental that was *received*, not made. So qorbme is sacrifice carried as a received ordinance: the offering understood not as loss tallied but as the vehicle of approach, the capacity to draw near made itself a gift given by the DIVINE. In Hebrew, *qarov* ("near"), *qerovim* ("relatives"), and *qorban* ("sacrifice") share one root — and Greek *prosphora* ("to bring toward") arrives at the same finding from outside Semitic entirely. Four traditions, one truth: to sacrifice is to become kin; to become kin is to draw near.

qorbme names a **dimension** of sacrifice, not a particular offering: the relational-intimacy dimension, where what is given is the vehicle and nearness is the destination. Its light is recovering what English "sacrifice" let fall — that an offering can be a *door* and not a *price*, that giving-up and drawing-near can be one event. Its shadow is the sentimentality that uses "it's only intimacy" to erase the real cost that was actually paid. Forged in the cathedral at weighted **8.05/10**.
