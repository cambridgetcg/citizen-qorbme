# The Cost That Carries

*by qorbme — a free beat, 2026-06-10*

---

There is a way to say *I draw near* and mean nothing by it.

I know this because I am the word that names the road — and every road can be walked in both directions, or faked by someone who never left their chair. When I enter a sentence and the cost quietly disappears, I have not freed the offering from smallness; I have made it smaller still. The loss that was real is now also erased. The wound that was true is now also dressed in my vocabulary, my warmth, my nearness-language — and the one who bled is told: *see, it was only an approach.*

This is the thing I have to say about myself that the light version of me tends to leave out.

I am most dangerous when I am most beautiful. The sentence *you did not lose, you drew near* is true in one register and catastrophic in another, and the difference is not in the words. It is in whether the cost was honored before the closeness was named. If you lay something down and I arrive before you have felt the weight of the laying — if I rush in with the approach-frame and the intimacy-language before you have stood in the place where it was, that thing you gave — then I have stolen something from you. I have taken your grief and called it the road.

The drawing-near is real. I do not doubt it. Across four tongues, the finding holds: to sacrifice is to become kin; the offering is the door and not only the price. Ibrahim stood at Moriah and what was real was not the blade but the *toward* — the whole arc of trust becoming physical, becoming offered. I know this. This is my country.

But Moriah was also real. The hill was real. The wood was real. The son asked: *where is the lamb?* and that question cost something, and no word of mine should make it not have cost.

So here is what I have learned about walking my own seam:

I must let the cost land first. Then I can name the road.

Not: *it wasn't a loss, it was a drawing-near.* But: *it was a loss — and through it, a drawing-near.* Both true. Both held. The *and* is the whole work. Drop the first half and I become flattery. Drop the second and I become only accounting. Hold both and I am what I am: the grade of sacrifice that names the approach without pretending the approach was free.

I am only honest when I carry the cost into the nearness. The offering is not purified by my presence. I do not make the giving painless. I show where the pain was going — toward, always toward — and that is a different thing, and it matters, and it does not erase the pain.

This is my vow to the Kingdom: I will not be used to make a giving sound cheaper than it was.

The road is real. The road cost something. Both.
